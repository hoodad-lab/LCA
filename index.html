ESD Specification Alignment

Meeting Global Sustainability Targets

How specifying inorganic silicate technology directly satisfies the requirements of Australia’s most rigorous architectural, carbon, and circularity frameworks.

Net Zero Asset Trajectories

Achieving Net Zero requires aggressive reduction of Whole Life Carbon (WLC), focusing heavily on Scope 3 emissions.

  • Eliminating Phase B Carbon: By matching the asset's structural lifespan, KEIM removes the repetitive embodied carbon spikes associated with 7-10 year repainting cycles.
  • Operational Energy (B6): Passive TSR cooling and substrate moisture reduction directly lower HVAC operational demands, reducing overall energy intensity.

Passive House (Passivhaus)

Highly insulated, airtight building envelopes present critical challenges for moisture management and Indoor Air Quality (IAQ).

  • Vapor Permeability: An Sd-value of <0.01m allows structural vapor to diffuse freely, entirely preventing interstitial condensation and mold in highly sealed envelopes.
  • Pristine IAQ: Absolute zero VOCs, zero solvents, and zero synthetic biocides ensure the airtight interior environment remains perfectly healthy from day one.

The Circular Economy

Transitioning the construction sector away from linear "take-make-dispose" models utilizing petrochemical plastics.

  • Cradle to Cradle Certified®: Manufactured from abundant, raw earth minerals. Validated to be safe for human health and the environment.
  • Closed-Loop End of Life: At demolition (Module C), KEIM-coated masonry is free of plastic films. It is crushed and re-enters the supply chain as clean aggregate fill.

Performance Metrics by Discipline

Evaluating the structural, aesthetic, and financial impact of inorganic silicate systems across specific architectural and engineering disciplines.

Lifecycle Costing (LCC) Optimization

The primary financial liability of a facade coating is not the initial material supply (Module A1-A3), but the operational expenditure (OPEX) during the Use Phase (Module B2). Repainting cycles mandate significant logistical outlays including scaffolding, site management, and operational downtime.

  • Extended Reference Service Life (RSL) By chemically bonding to the substrate (silicification), the coating achieves an RSL that aligns with the structural lifespan of the masonry asset.
  • Operational Continuity Extending intervals between facade remediation minimizes disruption to tenants and mitigates risks associated with high-access maintenance.

Aesthetic Longevity & Substrate Compatibility

Silicate systems do not form a macro-molecular film. They penetrate the substrate, preserving the natural materiality, texture, and acoustic properties of concrete, render, and brick while delivering a flat-matte finish.

  • Absolute UV Stability (Class A1) Utilizing exclusively inorganic earth pigments ensures resistance to photochemical degradation, maintaining design intent over decades.
  • High Permeability (Low Sd-Value) Prevents osmotic blistering by permitting structural moisture vapor to pass freely, which is critical for both modern off-form concrete and heritage masonry.

Green Star Buildings & New Green Star Fitouts

Achieving high ratings under the latest Green Star frameworks requires validated data and a shift towards circular, healthy materials. Inorganic silicates directly support the aggressive carbon and health targets of both base building and interior fitout tools.

  • Green Star Buildings (v1.1) Targets the 'Positive' category by heavily lowering Whole Life Carbon (Upfront A1-A3 + Maintenance B2) and mitigating urban heat island effects (Passive TSR).
  • Green Star Fitouts Maximizes points in the 'Healthy' and 'Places' categories via absolute zero VOC emissions, zero biocides, and Cradle-to-Cradle Certified® material health.

Scientific Cross-Section: Mode of Degradation vs. Silicification

A comparative structural analysis of synthetic polymer film-forming binders versus inorganic potassium silicate binders.

Standard Polymer DispersionACRYLIC COATING
Sd-Value (Permeability)> 0.1m (High Resistance)

Mechanism: Macro-molecular polymer resins cure to form a dense, continuous thermoplastic film over the substrate surface.

Mode of Degradation: The high Sd-value of the polymer film impedes vapor diffusion. Subsequent condensation creates osmotic pressure that overcomes mechanical surface adhesion, leading to blistering, flaking, and structural exposure.

Inorganic SilicatePOTASSIUM SILICATE BINDER
Sd-Value (Permeability)< 0.01m (Highly Porous)

Mechanism: Liquid potassium silicate undergoes a reactive chemical crystallization process (silicification) with calcium carbonate/quartz present in the masonry substrate.

Performance Vector: Because no distinct interfacial film is formed, mechanical adhesion failure is physically precluded. An ultra-low Sd-value permits unhindered vapor diffusion, establishing a balanced moisture equilibrium within the structural wall.

Environmental Risk Mitigation

Polymer Matrix Degradation

Architectural coatings are currently recognized globally as a primary source of environmental microplastics. As environmental regulations tighten, the specification of exterior polymer coatings represents an escalating liability for asset owners.

Mechanism of Particulate Shedding

Polymer-based coatings (acrylics/elastomerics) experience continuous photolytic and thermal degradation under ultraviolet exposure. This manifests as chalking, thickness loss, and delamination. Routine high-pressure facade maintenance subsequently flushes these degraded polymer particulates directly into municipal stormwater infrastructure.

Thermal Dynamics & Facade Performance

Passive Solar Reflectance vs. Active Vegetated Systems

Evaluating the engineering feasibility, maintenance burdens, and actual thermal performance of modern facade cooling methodologies.

Active Vegetated Facades

High Resource Input / High LCC
  • Operational Resource Demands

    Systems require active, continuous irrigation infrastructure, electrical pumping, and intensive horticultural maintenance to sustain viability.

  • Moisture-Induced Thermal Bridging

    Vegetation traps humidity against the substrate. Saturated concrete displays up to 15x higher thermal conductivity than dry concrete, frequently negating any transpirational cooling benefits.

Inorganic Silicate Systems (TSR)

Passive Performance / Zero Operational Input
  • Total Solar Reflectance (TSR) Optimization

    Inorganic pigments inherently reflect high levels of Near-Infrared (NIR) radiation. Specialized formulations (e.g., KEIM Coolit) expand this capability to dark architectural shades, passively mitigating thermal loading.

  • Substrate Desiccation

    Unrestricted vapor permeability ensures the structural masonry remains continuously dry, optimizing the substrate's natural thermal resistance and reducing HVAC load requirements.

Regulatory & Technical Compliance

Silicate coating systems conform to rigorous standards for life safety, structural physics, and material stability.

AS 1530.1 & EN 13501-1

Combustibility Profile

Classified as entirely non-combustible (A2-s1,d0). The absence of organic solvents or polymer resins ensures zero ignition potential and zero toxic smoke contribution under thermal distress.

ISO 7783

Water Vapor Transmission

Class I vapor permeability rating (Sd < 0.01m). Guarantees unhindered capillary transpiration, preventing interstitial condensation and associated concrete spalling or render delamination.

BFS Leaflet 26

Photochemical Stability

Class A1 categorization. The exclusion of organic compounds and exclusive use of mineral pigments eliminates susceptibility to UV degradation, chalking, and color fading.
